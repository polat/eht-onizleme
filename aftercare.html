Why it matters

Twelve months of aftercare is normal. Who does the checks is what counts.

UK clinics usually include a year of aftercare too. When you are treated abroad, the real questions are different: who looks at your eyes once you are home, how quickly, and what happens if you need more treatment.

Most problems after eye surgery are easy to treat when someone is checking. So every check is planned before you fly, each one has a named person or team behind it, and the rest is in writing.

Warning signs

Know what is normal, and when to get help fast.

Your surgeon gives you a written list for your treatment. These are the general signs.

Usually normal in the first weeks

  • Eyes that feel scratchy, watery or sensitive to light for the first hours or days
  • Halos or glare around lights at night, which usually fade over months
  • Dry eyes, helped by lubricating drops
  • Vision that is slightly hazy at first and sharpens over days or weeks
  • After CXL or Trans-PRK, soreness for the first few days

Get urgent care near you if you notice

  • A sudden drop in vision, in one or both eyes
  • Severe pain, or pain that gets worse instead of better
  • Redness that keeps increasing, especially with discharge
  • Flashes of light, a shower of new floaters, or a shadow or curtain across your vision, especially after lens surgery
Go to your nearest eye emergency service first, then message us. In the UK that is an eye casualty unit or A&E, and NHS 111 can tell you where to go. Do not wait for your next planned check. When you message us, your coordinator sends your surgery records to the doctor seeing you and tells your surgeon's team.
Your records

Before you fly home you get your records in English: for your own optician, your GP, or any doctor who sees you later. They show exactly what was done, so nobody has to guess.

Results from every check after you fly home are added, and you can ask your coordinator for copies at any time.

Questions

Aftercare questions, answered.

What does aftercare include?

Planned checks for 12 months after your treatment: a final check in Istanbul, video calls with your surgeon's team, and checks at a partner optometrist near you where available. You also get your medicines, an aftercare kit and your records in English.

Who checks my eyes after I fly home?

Your surgeon's team, by video, and a partner optometrist near you where we have one. Your coordinator books each check and keeps your records together.

What if there is no partner optometrist near me?

Your checks are done by video, and your own optician can check your vision and eye pressure using your records in English. Ask your coordinator before you book how this works where you live.

Are the checks included in the price?

Yes. The planned checks for 12 months are part of every package. [Whether extra, unplanned checks are included: to be confirmed by Veon]

What happens if I need a top-up treatment?

Your surgeon decides whether a top-up (enhancement) is safe and likely to help. The written enhancement and complication policy says who pays for the treatment and for travel. Read it before you book.

What should I do if something feels wrong at home?

If you notice a sudden drop in vision, severe pain, increasing redness, or flashes, floaters or a shadow after lens surgery, go to your nearest eye emergency service first, then message us. For anything less urgent, message your coordinator on WhatsApp and your surgeon's team will reply.

Can my own GP or optician see me?

Yes, and your records in English help them. Before you book, read the NHS guidance on treatment abroad, so you know what the NHS can and cannot do for you.

What happens after 12 months?

Your planned checks end at 12 months. After EVO ICL, a yearly check at an eye clinic near you is usually advised, and lens patients may need a short laser treatment for cloudy vision years later. Your records in English help any eye clinic.
