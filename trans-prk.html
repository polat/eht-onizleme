How it works

Surface laser. No cut, no flap.

Trans-PRK reshapes the front of your cornea from the outside. The laser clears the thin surface skin, called the epithelium, and reshapes the cornea underneath. The skin grows back within a few days.

  1. The laser clears the thin surface skin

    In one continuous treatment, it removes the epithelium over the area it will reshape. No blade touches your eye and no flap is made, which is why it is called no-touch.

  2. It reshapes the cornea underneath

    The same laser removes a precise amount of tissue, planned from your scans, so your cornea focuses light correctly. It treats short-sight, long-sight or astigmatism.

  3. A soft bandage lens protects the eye while it heals

    A clear contact lens sits on each eye for the first few days while the skin regrows. Your surgeon removes it at a check-up in Istanbul.

Is it for me?

Who Trans-PRK suits, and who it does not.

Your surgeon decides after scanning your corneas. These are the usual limits, so you know where you stand before you send anything.

Usually a good fit if you

  • Are 18 or over and your prescription has been stable for at least a year
  • Are short-sighted, long-sighted or have astigmatism, within the range your surgeon confirms from your scans
  • Have been told your corneas are on the thin side for LASIK: with no flap, Trans-PRK often still works
  • Play contact sports or martial arts, or work somewhere an eye could be knocked
  • Can allow 4–5 nights in Istanbul and a slower first week

Something else may suit you better if you

  • Need clear vision within a day or two: SMILE Pro or Femto-LASIK recovers faster
  • Have a very high prescription or very thin corneas: EVO ICL is often the answer
  • Are over 45 and need reading glasses: laser does not fix reading vision, lens replacement can
  • Have keratoconus or early signs of it: cross-linking comes first
  • Are pregnant or breastfeeding, or have an untreated eye condition: wait, or talk to your surgeon

Risks

Risks and side effects, explained plainly.

Every eye operation carries some risk. Your surgeon goes through yours in writing, in your language, before you book.

Common, temporary

Pain in the first days

Expected while the surface regrows, and more than after LASIK or SMILE Pro. It usually eases after 3–4 days; drops and pain relief help.

Common, temporary

Dry eyes

Usually settles within months with lubricating drops. Typically milder than after LASIK.

Common, temporary

Halos and glare at night

Rings around lights are normal at first and usually fade over 3–6 months.

Uncommon

Corneal haze

A faint cloudiness as the surface heals, which can blur vision. Your surgeon looks for it at your checks and treats it with drops if needed.

Uncommon

Under- or over-correction

A few people need a small top-up treatment once their vision has fully settled.

Rare

Infection or inflammation

The healing surface needs clean hands and careful drops. Treated with drops when caught early, which is why the checks in Istanbul matter.

Your surgeon will tell you if your exam shows a higher risk, even if it means no treatment.

Questions

Trans-PRK questions, answered.

How much does Trans-PRK cost in Turkey?

With Eye Health Turkey, Trans-PRK costs from £825 per eye, or £1,650 for both eyes. That includes your exam, treatment, 4–5 hotel nights, transfers and aftercare checks. UK clinics publish surface laser prices of £549 to £2,950 per eye, so compare what each price covers for your prescription.

What is the difference between Trans-PRK, PRK and LASEK?

All three are surface laser treatments with no flap. In PRK and LASEK the surface skin is removed first, with an instrument or a mild alcohol solution, and then the laser reshapes the cornea. In Trans-PRK the laser does both steps, which is why it is called no-touch.

Is Trans-PRK painful?

Not during treatment: numbing drops are used, and you feel pressure rather than pain. The first 3–4 days afterwards are sore, gritty and watery while the surface regrows. Your surgeon prescribes drops and pain relief for this.

How long does Trans-PRK take to heal?

The surface heals in a few days, when the bandage lenses come out. Vision clears over 1–2 weeks and keeps sharpening for several weeks.

Is Trans-PRK better than LASIK?

Neither is better for everyone. Trans-PRK has no flap, so it suits thinner corneas and contact sports, and usually causes less dryness. Femto-LASIK recovers faster: most people see clearly the next day. Your surgeon recommends one after your scans.

Why do I need 4–5 nights in Istanbul?

So your surgeon can check your eyes while the surface heals, remove the bandage lenses once it has, and confirm you can fly. If your eyes need an extra day, we extend your stay.

When can I go back to work and drive?

Most people manage desk work after about a week, with screen breaks. You can drive once your vision meets the legal standard, which your check-up confirms. Jobs with dust, water or physical contact may need longer.

Is Trans-PRK good for thin corneas?

Often, because no flap is made, so more of the cornea is left untouched. Your scans show whether there is enough thickness for your prescription. If not, EVO ICL may suit you.

Can I play contact sports after Trans-PRK?

Yes, usually after about 2 weeks, once your surgeon agrees. With no flap, there is nothing to dislodge later, which is why it is often chosen for boxing, rugby and martial arts.

How long do the results last?

The correction is permanent. Your eyes still age, though: most people need reading glasses from their mid-forties, whether or not they had laser.
