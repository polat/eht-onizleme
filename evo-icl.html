EVO ICL lens surgery in Istanbul

EVO ICL is a thin, flexible lens placed inside your eye, in front of your natural lens, to correct short-sight and astigmatism. No tissue is removed, and the lens can be taken out later if needed. It is often the answer when your prescription is beyond the laser range or your corneas are too thin. With us it costs from £1,475 per eye, including your exam, hotel, transfers and aftercare.

How it works

A lens inside the eye. Nothing removed.

Short-sight happens when the eye focuses light too strongly. Laser corrects this by reshaping the cornea. EVO ICL adds a thin corrective lens inside the eye instead, so the cornea stays as it is.

  1. Your lens is chosen from your measurements

    Before you travel, the space inside the front of your eye and your prescription are measured. Your lens size is chosen to fit. Toric lenses for astigmatism are often made to order.

  2. Your surgeon places it through a small opening

    The eye is numbed. The folded lens goes in at the edge of the cornea and unfolds behind the iris, the coloured part of the eye, just in front of your own lens.

  3. Your natural lens and cornea stay

    Nothing is cut away. If it is ever needed, a surgeon can take the lens out or replace it.

Is it for me?

Who EVO ICL suits, and who it does not.

Your surgeon decides after measuring your eyes. These are the usual limits, so you know where you stand before you send anything.

Usually a good fit if you

  • Are 21–45 and your prescription has been stable for at least a year
  • Are short-sighted from about −3 to −20, with or without astigmatism up to about 4.00
  • Have been told your prescription is beyond the laser range, or your corneas are too thin for laser
  • Prefer a treatment that removes no tissue and can be taken out later if needed
  • Have enough room in the front of your eye and a healthy cell count in your cornea, both measured before you travel

Something else may suit you better if you

  • Are over 45 and need reading glasses: an ICL does not fix reading vision, lens replacement can
  • Have a prescription within the laser range and healthy corneas: SMILE Pro or Femto-LASIK may be simpler
  • Have keratoconus that is still changing: cross-linking comes first
  • Are under 21: your prescription may still be changing
  • Are pregnant or breastfeeding, or have an untreated eye condition: wait, or talk to your surgeon

Risks

Risks and side effects, explained plainly.

EVO ICL is surgery inside the eye, so it carries different risks from laser. The lens can be taken out, but that is another operation. Your surgeon goes through your risks in writing, in your language, before you book.

Common at first

Halos and glare at night

Some people notice rings around lights at night in the first weeks. This usually becomes less noticeable over time.

Uncommon

Raised eye pressure

This is why your pressure is checked a few hours after surgery and again the next day. A rise found early is treated straight away.

Uncommon

The lens sits too close or too far

The gap between the ICL and your natural lens is checked after surgery. If it is not right, your surgeon may reposition or replace the lens.

Rare

Cataract

A clouding of your natural lens. It has been reported only rarely with the EVO lens. If it happens, it is treated with cataract surgery.

Very rare

Infection inside the eye

Serious, but very rare. Drops before and after surgery lower the risk, and your early checks look for it.

Long term

Loss of corneal cells

The cornea's inner layer loses cells slowly with age, and a lens inside the eye can add to this. That is why your cell count is checked every year.

Your surgeon will tell you if your measurements show a higher risk, even if it means no treatment.

Questions

EVO ICL questions, answered.

How much does EVO ICL cost in Turkey?

With Eye Health Turkey, EVO ICL costs from £1,475 per eye, or £2,950 for both eyes. That includes your exam, the lens and surgery, your hotel, transfers and aftercare checks. UK clinics publish ICL prices of £3,395 to £4,600 per eye. A toric lens for astigmatism can cost more.

What is the difference between ICL and EVO ICL?

ICL stands for implantable collamer lens, a lens that sits inside the eye in front of your natural lens. EVO and EVO+ are the current models made by STAAR Surgical. Your written plan names the exact lens.

Is EVO ICL better than laser eye surgery?

Neither is better for everyone. Laser is usually simpler when your prescription is within range and your corneas are healthy. EVO ICL is often chosen for stronger prescriptions or thin corneas, because it does not reshape the cornea. Your surgeon recommends one after your measurements.

Can the lens be removed?

Yes. A surgeon can take the lens out or replace it if it is ever needed. That is another operation inside the eye, with its own risks, so it is not something to plan on.

Does it hurt?

The eye is numbed, and your surgeon explains the anaesthetic before the day. Most people feel pressure rather than pain. Afterwards the eye can feel scratchy and vision is blurry for a while.

Will I feel the lens, or will anyone see it?

The lens sits behind the iris, so other people cannot see it. Once your eye has healed, you should not feel it.

How long does the lens last?

It is designed to stay in your eye long term. Yearly checks of your eye pressure, corneal cells and lens position make sure it stays well placed. If you need cataract surgery later in life, the lens is removed then.

Why are my eyes measured before I travel?

Your lens size is chosen from the measurements, and toric lenses are often made to order. Measuring early means the right lens is ready when you arrive, and your exam in Istanbul confirms the numbers.

Are both eyes done on the same day?

Usually one eye at a time, a day apart, so the first eye can be checked before the second. Some surgeons treat both eyes on the same day. Your surgeon plans this with you at your video call.

Will I still need reading glasses?

Probably, later in life. An ICL corrects distance vision, but it does not stop the natural ageing of your lens. Most people need reading glasses from their mid-forties.
