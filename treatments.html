The usual starting points

  • 18 or over, with a stable prescription: laser, such as SMILE Pro, Femto-LASIK or Trans-PRK
  • A high prescription or thin corneas: EVO ICL, a lens placed inside the eye
  • Over 45 and need reading glasses: lens replacement can correct near and far
  • Cloudy, dim or glary vision: often a cataract, treated with cataract surgery
  • Keratoconus: cross-linking to slow it down comes first

Not yet, or not without more checks, if you

  • Are under 18, or your prescription has changed in the last year
  • Are pregnant or breastfeeding: your prescription can change for a while
  • Have an eye condition, or a health condition that affects healing

Keratoconus: cross-linking to slow it down.

Keratoconus makes the cornea thin and bulge over time. Cross-linking (CXL) strengthens the cornea to slow or stop that change, rather than to sharpen your vision. It is often done young, and progress is checked with scans for a year.

Who is not suitable

Who is not suitable for eye surgery.

Some people are better waiting, and some are better with a different treatment. These are the general reasons. Your surgeon checks each one at your exam and tells you if surgery is not right for you, even if it means no treatment.

Under 18, or a changing prescription

Eyes usually keep changing into early adulthood. Laser and lens surgery wait until you are 18 or over and your prescription has been stable for at least a year.

Pregnant or breastfeeding

Your prescription and your eyes can change during this time, so surgeons usually ask you to wait. Your surgeon will tell you how long.

Keratoconus or thin corneas

Laser is usually not advised, because it removes corneal tissue. Cross-linking for keratoconus, or EVO ICL for thin corneas, may be options instead.

About keratoconus

Some eye conditions

For example severe dry eye, an infection or inflammation, glaucoma or a retinal problem. Some can be treated first; some rule a treatment out. Your exam checks for them.

Some general health conditions

Conditions and medicines that affect healing or the immune system, such as poorly controlled diabetes. Tell your surgeon about every condition and medicine.

Short-sighted and under 50, for lens replacement

Lens replacement carries a higher risk of retinal detachment in younger short-sighted people. EVO ICL or laser is usually advised instead.

Questions

Choosing a treatment, answered.

Which eye treatment is right for me?

It depends on your age, your prescription and your corneas. As a rule of thumb: laser for adults with a stable prescription, EVO ICL for high prescriptions or thin corneas, lens replacement after 45 if you need reading glasses, cataract surgery for a cloudy lens, and cross-linking for keratoconus. A surgeon confirms which suits you after reviewing your prescription and scans.

What is the difference between laser and lens surgery?

Laser reshapes the clear front of the eye, the cornea. Lens surgery works inside the eye: EVO ICL adds a thin lens in front of your own, while lens replacement and cataract surgery replace your own lens with an artificial one.

Can I have laser eye surgery after 40?

Often, yes, for distance vision. Laser does not stop the need for reading glasses that comes with age, which most people notice from their mid-forties. After 45, lens replacement can correct reading and distance vision together.

My prescription is very high. What are my options?

Above about −10, a lens placed inside the eye (EVO ICL) usually gives a better result than laser. Between −6 and −10, laser may still work if your corneas are thick enough. Your scans decide.

Is lens replacement the same as cataract surgery?

The operation is very similar: your own lens is replaced with an artificial one. Cataract surgery is done because the lens has become cloudy. Lens replacement is done to reduce your need for glasses, usually after 45.

Are both eyes treated on the same day?

With laser, yes, in one session. With lens surgery, the two eyes are often treated on consecutive days. Your surgeon decides.

Which treatment needs the shortest stay?

SMILE Pro, with 2–3 nights in Istanbul. Femto-LASIK needs 3 nights, Trans-PRK and EVO ICL 4–5, lens replacement and cataract surgery 4–6, and cross-linking 5–7.

What if I am told I am not suitable?

Your surgeon explains why and offers an alternative if one suits your eyes. If none does, your deposit is refunded in full. No surgeon can promise a result, and a good one will tell you if surgery is not right for you.
