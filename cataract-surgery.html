How it works

Cloudy lens out, clear lens in.

A cataract is a clouding of the lens inside your eye. It builds up slowly, so vision becomes dim or hazy and lights start to dazzle. Glasses cannot clear it. Replacing the lens can.

  1. Measurements choose your new lens

    On day 1, scans measure your eye (biometry), map your cornea and check your retina (OCT). Your surgeon uses them to choose the lens type and its power.

  2. Your surgeon removes the cloudy lens through a small opening

    Under numbing drops, the cloudy lens is gently broken up and removed. The folded artificial lens opens in the same natural pocket, called the capsule.

  3. A clear lens, one eye at a time

    The second eye is usually treated a day or two later. Vision is often blurry at first and clears over the following days and weeks.

Is it for me?

Who cataract surgery with a premium lens suits.

Your surgeon decides after examining your eyes. These are the usual signs and limits, so you know where you stand before you send anything.

Usually a good fit if you

  • Have been told you have a cataract, or notice cloudy or dim vision
  • Are bothered by glare from headlights or sunlight, or colours look faded
  • Find your glasses help less than they used to
  • Would like a premium lens, so you need glasses less after surgery
  • Have astigmatism: a toric lens can correct it at the same time

Something else may suit you better if you

  • Have no cataract yet but want fewer glasses: see lens replacement
  • Have glaucoma, macular degeneration or diabetic eye disease: a premium lens may not suit, and your surgeon needs your records first
  • Are happy with a standard lens and can have surgery near home: that may be the simpler route, see the comparison
  • Have health problems that make travel hard, or cannot stay 4–6 nights: surgery close to home may suit you better

Recovery

What the first weeks feel like.

Recovery is gradual: each eye settles over days, and with a premium lens your brain adjusts to it over weeks. This is what is typical. Your surgeon gives you your own written plan.

  1. First few hours

    Blurry, bright and a little gritty

    Rest at the hotel. Your pupil is still wide from the drops, so light feels bright. Sunglasses help.

  2. Next day

    Clearer, though still settling

    Your first eye is checked before the second is treated. Vision can stay hazy for a few days.

    Watching TVShort walksNo rubbing
  3. First week

    Drops, care and flying home

    Use your drops on time and keep soap and water out of your eyes. Flying is fine once your surgeon confirms it.

    Flying homeReading and screensNo swimmingNo eye make-up
  4. Weeks 1–4

    Back to most of everyday life

    Gardening, driving and exercise return as your vision and checks allow. Your surgeon tells you when each one is safe for you.

  5. Months 1–3

    Vision settles

    Colours may look brighter than you remember. With a trifocal, halos usually become less noticeable. If you still need glasses for some tasks, your optician can prescribe them once your eyes have settled.

  6. Months or years later

    A haze behind the lens can be cleared

    If your vision slowly clouds again, a quick laser called YAG capsulotomy clears it, usually near home.

Risks

Risks and side effects, explained plainly.

Cataract surgery is an operation inside the eye, so it carries some risk. Your surgeon goes through yours in writing, in your language, before you book.

Common, temporary

Gritty, dry or blurry first days

Normal while the eye heals. Lubricating drops help, and vision usually clears over the first days and weeks.

Common with trifocals

Halos and glare at night

Rings around lights are common with trifocal lenses at first, and less so with EDOF and monofocal lenses. They usually become less noticeable with time.

Uncommon

A small leftover prescription

Some people still need glasses for certain tasks, especially if another eye condition limits what a lens can do. Your surgeon will tell you the options.

Can develop later

Haze behind the lens

Called posterior capsule opacification. The thin capsule holding your lens can cloud over months or years later. A quick laser clears it, usually near home.

Rare

Retinal detachment

The retina can come away from the back of the eye. It needs urgent treatment, so you get a written plan for the warning signs: flashes, new floaters or a shadow.

Rare

Infection or inflammation

Infection inside the eye is rare but serious. Antibiotic drops and your checks in Istanbul are there to catch any problem early.

Your surgeon will tell you if your exam shows a higher risk, even if it means no treatment.

Questions

Cataract surgery questions, answered.

How much does cataract surgery cost in Turkey?

With Eye Health Turkey, cataract surgery with a premium lens (toric, EDOF or trifocal) costs from £1,600 per eye, or £3,200 for both eyes. That includes your exam, surgery, lens, hotel for the nights in your plan, transfers and aftercare checks. UK private clinics publish premium-lens cataract prices of £3,400 to £5,000 per eye.

Can I get a premium lens on the NHS?

The NHS offers cataract surgery with a standard lens, at no charge to you. You cannot pay the NHS extra to upgrade to a toric, EDOF or trifocal lens, so premium lenses are only available privately. Rules can differ between the four UK nations; your GP or optometrist can tell you how it works where you live.

My insurance at home covers cataract surgery. Is it worth travelling?

It depends on what you want from the lens. In Germany, statutory health insurance covers standard cataract surgery, and patients pay only the extra for a premium lens, typically €1,000–2,500 per eye. If a standard lens is all you need, surgery close to home may be the simpler choice. Compare like for like, including flights and time away.

What is the difference between monofocal, toric, EDOF and trifocal lenses?

A monofocal lens focuses at one distance, usually far, so most people wear glasses for reading. An EDOF lens covers far to middle, with fewer halos than a trifocal. A trifocal is designed for near, middle and far. Toric versions of each also correct astigmatism.

When is a cataract ready to be removed?

You do not need to wait for a cataract to 'ripen'. Surgery is usually considered when it gets in the way of daily life, such as reading, driving or coping with bright light. Your surgeon will advise after your exam.

Are both eyes done on the same trip, and how long do I stay?

Usually yes: one eye at a time, a day or two apart, so your surgeon can check the first eye before treating the second. Most people stay 4–6 nights. If only one eye needs surgery, your stay is shorter, and your plan shows the exact nights.

Does cataract surgery hurt?

Numbing drops are used, and most people feel light pressure rather than pain. Afterwards your eye can feel gritty and look slightly red for a few days.

Can a cataract come back?

No. The cataract is removed with your natural lens. Months or years later, the thin capsule behind the new lens can turn hazy. A quick laser treatment clears it, usually near home.

Is laser cataract surgery better?

For routine cataracts, large studies have not shown a clear extra benefit from laser-assisted surgery over the standard method. Your surgeon will tell you which method they use and why.

I have glaucoma or macular degeneration. Can I still have it?

Many people with other eye conditions have cataract surgery, but the condition can change which lens suits you, and a trifocal may not be advised. Send your eye reports first, and a surgeon will review them before anything is planned.
