How it works

Two lasers. One thin flap.

Short-sight, long-sight and astigmatism happen when the cornea does not focus light exactly on the back of the eye. Femto-LASIK reshapes the cornea so it does.

  1. A femtosecond laser makes a thin flap

    It creates a thin flap in the front layer of your cornea, left attached at one edge like a hinge. Your surgeon gently folds it back. No blade touches your eye.

  2. An excimer laser reshapes the cornea underneath

    It removes a precise amount of tissue, planned from your scans, so your cornea focuses light correctly. The same step treats short-sight, long-sight or astigmatism.

  3. The flap is laid back and seals by itself

    No stitches are needed. Vision is usually clear enough for everyday life the next morning and keeps sharpening over the following weeks.

Is it for me?

Who Femto-LASIK suits, and who it does not.

Your surgeon decides after scanning your corneas. These are the usual limits, so you know where you stand before you send anything.

Usually a good fit if you

  • Are 18 or over and your prescription has been stable for at least a year
  • Are short-sighted, long-sighted or have astigmatism, within the range your surgeon confirms from your scans
  • Have healthy corneas of normal thickness, checked on your scans
  • Want clear vision quickly: most people see well enough for everyday life the next day
  • Have no more than mild dry eyes: your exam includes a dry-eye check

Something else may suit you better if you

  • Have a very high prescription or thin corneas: EVO ICL or Trans-PRK is often the answer
  • Play contact sports or martial arts: SMILE Pro or Trans-PRK has no flap to knock
  • Are over 45 and need reading glasses: laser does not fix reading vision, lens replacement can
  • Have keratoconus or early signs of it: cross-linking comes first
  • Are pregnant or breastfeeding, or have an untreated eye condition: wait, or talk to your surgeon

Risks

Risks and side effects, explained plainly.

Every eye operation carries some risk. Your surgeon goes through yours in writing, in your language, before you book.

Common, temporary

Dry eyes

In a large US study (PROWL), about 3 in 10 people with no dry eyes before LASIK noticed new dryness in the first 3 months. It usually settles with lubricating drops.

Common, temporary

Halos and glare at night

Rings around lights are normal at first and usually fade over 3–6 months.

Uncommon

Flap problems

The flap can wrinkle or shift, most often after a knock in the first weeks. Your surgeon can usually smooth or reposition it, which is why you avoid rubbing and contact sport.

Uncommon

Under- or over-correction

A few people need a small top-up treatment once their vision has settled, usually after about 3 months.

Rare

Infection or inflammation

Treated with drops when caught early, which is why the next-day check matters.

Rare

Weakening of the cornea

Called ectasia, reported in about 0.04–0.6% of LASIK eyes. Your scans before treatment screen for the risk, and you are not treated if it is raised.

Your surgeon will tell you if your exam shows a higher risk, even if it means no treatment.

Questions

Femto-LASIK questions, answered.

How much does LASIK cost in Turkey?

With Eye Health Turkey, Femto-LASIK costs from £750 per eye, or £1,500 for both eyes. That includes your exam, treatment, three hotel nights, transfers and aftercare checks. UK clinics publish LASIK prices of £1,295 to £3,750 per eye.

What is the difference between LASIK and Femto-LASIK?

Both lift a thin flap and reshape the cornea underneath. In older LASIK the flap is cut with a fine blade. In Femto-LASIK a femtosecond laser makes the flap, so both steps are done by laser. That is why it is sometimes called bladeless LASIK.

Is Femto-LASIK better than SMILE Pro?

Neither is better for everyone. Femto-LASIK also treats long-sight, and vision is usually clear the next day. SMILE Pro has no flap, so it suits contact sports and usually causes less dryness. Your surgeon recommends one after your scans.

Does LASIK hurt?

Numbing drops are used, so most people feel pressure rather than pain for a short time. Afterwards your eyes feel scratchy and watery for a few hours.

Can LASIK correct long-sight and astigmatism?

Yes, within limits. Your surgeon checks your prescription and corneal thickness to confirm it is within laser range. Strong long-sight may be better treated with a lens, and your surgeon will tell you if so.

How long do the results last?

The correction is permanent. Your eyes still age, though: most people need reading glasses from their mid-forties, whether or not they had laser.

When can I fly home after LASIK?

On day 4 of your trip, after your surgeon has checked your eyes on day 3. Flying does not affect healing.

When can I drive and go back to work?

Most people return to desk work after 2–3 days. You can drive once your vision meets the legal standard, which your check-up confirms, often within the first week. Jobs with dust, water or physical contact may need longer.

Can I have LASIK with thin corneas?

Often not, because the flap uses part of the cornea's thickness. Trans-PRK, which has no flap, or EVO ICL, which leaves the cornea untouched, may suit you instead. Your scans decide.

Is it worth going abroad for LASIK?

It can be, if you compare like for like: the same type of laser, an experienced surgeon, and proper checks once you are home. Add flights and time off to our price, and check who looks after you if you need help later.
