Corneal cross-linking (CXL)

Cross-linking for keratoconus in Istanbul

Cross-linking uses riboflavin eye drops and UV light to strengthen the cornea, to slow or stop keratoconus getting worse. It protects the vision you have: it does not reverse the cone, and you will most likely still need glasses or contact lenses. With us it costs from £1,400 per eye, including your exam, hotel, transfers and a year of scan reviews.

How it works

Drops and light that make the cornea stronger.

In keratoconus, the cornea, the clear window at the front of the eye, slowly thins and bulges into a cone. Cross-linking adds new links between its fibres, so it is stiffer and less likely to keep changing.

  1. Numbing drops, then the surface is prepared

    In the standard method, called epi-off, the thin outer skin of the cornea is gently removed so the drops can soak in. It grows back within days.

  2. Riboflavin drops soak into the cornea

    Riboflavin is vitamin B2. The drops are given over a period your surgeon sets, while you lie back and rest.

  3. UV light links the fibres together

    A controlled UV light activates the riboflavin, which forms new links in the cornea. A bandage contact lens then protects the eye while the surface heals.

Is it for me?

When cross-linking is used, and when it is not.

Your surgeon decides after comparing your corneal scans over time. These are the usual situations, so you and your family know where you stand.

Usually considered if you

  • Have keratoconus confirmed on corneal scans (topography)
  • Have scans, or glasses and contact lenses that keep changing, showing it is getting worse
  • Are in your teens, twenties or early thirties, when keratoconus is most likely to change
  • Have a cornea thick enough to treat, measured at your exam
  • Want to protect the vision you have, knowing you may still need glasses or contact lenses

Something else may come first if you

  • Have not had corneal scans yet: start with a scan, or send your reports for a second opinion
  • Have keratoconus that has stayed stable for years: regular scans may be enough, and EVO ICL can improve vision once it is stable
  • Want sharper vision without glasses: cross-linking alone is not designed for that
  • Have advanced keratoconus with scarring: your surgeon may discuss other treatments, including a corneal transplant in some cases
  • Are pregnant or breastfeeding, or have an eye infection or untreated eye condition: wait, or talk to your surgeon

Risks

Risks and side effects, explained plainly.

Every eye treatment carries some risk. Your surgeon goes through yours in writing, in your language, before you book, with a parent present for under-18s.

Common, temporary

Pain in the first days

With the epi-off method, the eye is sore while the surface grows back. Pain relief and the bandage lens help.

Common, temporary

Blurry or changing vision

Sight can be worse than before for a while, and vary from day to day. It settles gradually over the following weeks and months.

Common, usually fades

Haze in the cornea

A faint cloudiness that your surgeon can see on checks. It usually fades over the following months.

Common

New glasses or lens fitting

As the cornea settles, your prescription may change. Your optician updates your glasses or refits your contact lenses.

In some people

Keratoconus keeps progressing

Cross-linking does not stop it in everyone. Your scans over 12 months show this, and your surgeon explains the next step.

Rare

Infection

The surface is open until it heals, so drops and the bandage lens protect it. This is why your check before flying matters.

Your surgeon will tell you if your scans show a higher risk, for example a cornea that is too thin, even if it means no treatment.

Questions

Keratoconus and cross-linking questions, answered.

How much does cross-linking cost in Turkey?

With Eye Health Turkey, cross-linking costs from £1,400 per eye. That includes your exam and corneal scans, the treatment, your hotel, transfers and a year of scan reviews. UK private clinics publish prices from £1,800 per eye. It is priced per eye because each eye is assessed on its own.

Will cross-linking improve my vision?

It is not designed to. Its aim is to stop keratoconus getting worse, so you keep the vision you have. You will most likely still need glasses or contact lenses. Once the cornea is stable, other treatments can sometimes improve vision, such as ring segments, topography-guided PRK or EVO ICL.

Should I be worried about keratoconus?

It is natural to worry, especially as a parent. Keratoconus affects the cornea, the clear window at the front of the eye. It can blur and distort vision, and it tends to change most in the teens and twenties. Finding it early matters, because cross-linking aims to keep the cornea as it is now. Your surgeon explains what your scans show and what, if anything, needs doing.

What age can my child have cross-linking?

There is no single age. Cross-linking is often considered in teenagers when scans show keratoconus is getting worse, because it tends to change faster when you are young. The surgeon decides on timing after reviewing the scans. A parent or guardian takes part in every step and gives consent [age rules to be confirmed by Veon].

What is the difference between epi-off and epi-on?

In the standard method, epi-off, the thin outer skin of the cornea is removed so the riboflavin can soak in. It grows back within days, which is why the first days are sore and a bandage lens is used. Some clinics also offer methods that leave the surface in place, called epi-on. Your surgeon tells you which method they use, and why it suits your eyes [methods offered to be confirmed by Veon].

Does cross-linking hurt?

Numbing drops are used, so the treatment itself is not usually painful. With the epi-off method, the eye is sore, watery and sensitive to light for the first few days while the surface heals. You have pain relief, drops and a bandage lens to help.

Are both eyes treated?

Only the eyes that need it. Keratoconus often affects both eyes, but not always at the same pace. From your scans, your surgeon decides whether to treat one or both eyes on this trip, and when. The price is per eye.

Can I keep wearing contact lenses?

You need to leave them out for a while before your exam, because they can change the shape of the cornea on the scans. Rigid lenses usually need longer. After treatment, your surgeon tells you when they can go back in, and your optician may need to refit them.

How do we know if it has worked?

Your corneal scans at 1, 3, 6 and 12 months are compared with the ones taken before treatment. If the shape has stopped changing, the treatment has done its job. Your surgeon reviews each scan and explains it to you.

Will I need more treatment later?

Sometimes. In some people keratoconus keeps progressing after cross-linking, and your surgeon will explain the next step. Others choose a treatment to improve vision once the cornea is stable. Any next step comes with its own written plan and price.
