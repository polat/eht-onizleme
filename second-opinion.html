Asking is normal

A second opinion is a normal part of a big decision.

The NHS says healthcare professionals rarely refuse a request for a second opinion, and UK General Medical Council guidance says doctors should respect a patient's wish to seek one.

A second opinion is not about proving anyone wrong. It is a second look at the same records. It may confirm what you were told, or show you options you had not heard about. Either way, you decide with a clearer head.

  • No referral needed to send us your records
  • Your first doctor is not contacted unless you ask us to
  • The answer is yours to share with anyone you like
What to send

Send what you have. A clear phone photo is a good start.

The surgeon can only review what is in front of them, so send everything you have, even if it looks technical. Photograph each page flat, in good light.

  • Reports and letters: what you were told, and by whom
  • Scans: corneal maps (topography), OCT scans or any printouts
  • Your glasses prescription, and contact lens details if you wear them
  • Your medicines, including any eye drops
  • Your questions, in your own words

Can and cannot

What a records review can do, and what it cannot.

A records review helps you understand your situation and your choices. It does not replace an eye exam.

A records review can

  • Explain in plain words what your reports and scans show
  • Say which options may suit you, and which probably do not
  • Point out what is missing, such as a scan that would help
  • Give you clear questions to ask your own doctor
  • Tell you if waiting, or no treatment for now, is an option

It cannot

  • Replace an eye exam. If you go ahead, your surgeon confirms everything in person before anything is done
  • Confirm that you are suitable for any treatment
  • Judge your first doctor. It is a second look at the same records, to help you decide
  • Promise a result. No one can promise the outcome of eye surgery
Sudden change in vision, pain or a red eye? Do not wait for a review. Go to your nearest eye emergency service now. In the UK that is an eye casualty unit or A&E, and NHS 111 can tell you where to go.

Questions

Second opinion questions, answered.

Who reviews my records?

An eye surgeon at our partner hospital in Istanbul, licensed in Türkiye. Your answer gives their name. We are the facilitator, not the doctor: we organise the review, but we do not give medical advice.

Is the review independent?

The surgeon who reviews your records works at our partner hospital, and the hospital pays us a commission if you choose treatment there. We tell you this so you can weigh the answer. The review comes with no obligation, and you can have treatment at home or anywhere else.

What should I send?

Any eye reports or letters, scans such as corneal maps or OCT, your glasses prescription and a list of your eye drops and medicines. A clear phone photo of each page is a good start.

My records are not in English. Is that a problem?

No. Send them in any language, as photos or PDFs. Your coordinator tells you if anything needs translating.

How long does it take?

[Time to a written answer: to be confirmed by Veon]. Your coordinator tells you when to expect it, and asks for anything that is missing.

Can a records review tell me I am suitable for surgery?

No. It can tell you which options may suit you, based on your records. Only a full eye exam can confirm that, and if you go ahead, your surgeon does it in person before anything is done.

Can I talk to the surgeon?

Yes. Your answer comes in writing, and you can ask for a video call to talk it through.

Do I have to have treatment in Türkiye afterwards?

No. The answer is yours to keep and to share with your own doctor. Treat at home, in Türkiye, or not at all: it is your decision.

Who sees my records?

Only your coordinator and the surgeon who reviews them, under your written consent, which covers sharing them with the partner hospital in Turkey. Never advertisers. The privacy notice explains your rights.
